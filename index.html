Before the apps

Three stories

Vodafone Ireland A television business from a standing start

Vodafone Ireland had no television business. I led the product build and launch from a standing start, covering the proposition, technology, hardware, content and streaming, and it grew to €25M+ in annual revenue. After launch I was asked to lead the whole connected home: television, fibre broadband and consumer devices.

M1 The first audit of a whole portfolio

At M1, a Singapore operator, I led a 23-person product organisation and a portfolio of 30 products. I ran the first audit of the whole portfolio, retired about 40% of it and moved those product managers onto growth products. The new 5G, cloud and Internet of Things products we then launched delivered S$7M in additional revenue.

Etisalat A new business in a field new to me

At Etisalat I was asked to work out whether AI mission-critical networks could become a new business, a field I had never worked in. Within two months I was the subject lead, and I led a cross-functional squad from a blank sheet to an investment case approved by the chief executive. It is now a dedicated department with its own budget and headcount.

How I work

Each new domain is a mini PhD

I treat each new domain as a mini PhD. When Etisalat asked me to work on mission-critical communications I had never worked in the field; within two months I was the subject lead. I keep learning in my own time: 17 online specialisations since 2020, with universities including Stanford, INSEAD, LMU Munich and Glasgow, most of them alongside a full-time role.

I also build. In 2026 I designed and built 14 apps as the sole developer, working with AI coding tools, and ten are on the App Store. Each app starts from written requirements and keeps a dated log of decisions, and its AI features are tested against fixed cases, with the results deciding what ships. So when I judge what AI can do for a business, I have already tried it myself.
